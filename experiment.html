02 / KEEP BOTH SIGNALS THROUGH THE WHOLE TASK

Let the participant
set the pace.

Ten to fifteen minutes is an estimate. Start collection before the task, and stop it after the participant finishes or withdraws.

A

Check contact while using the VR controls

  1. Keep the same MOND ear, GSR sites, sampling settings and processing version used at rest. Keep Shimmer on battery and off the dock.
  2. Fit the headset without pressing on the ear clip. Secure cables so turning and reaching do not pull the sensors or obstruct the participant.
  3. Pilot the actual controller grip. Two finger electrodes on a gripping hand may move or change pressure. If you cannot keep contact stable, resolve the mounting with the equipment guidance before collecting comparable sessions. Do not switch electrode sites halfway through a paired test.
  4. Check live signals during representative movement. Log dropouts and contact changes; a filter cannot prove a movement-related peak is psychological.

Electrode motion and pressure affect conductance: Shimmer GSR+ guide, §4.3 ↗

B

Prepare the VR recording in iMotions

  1. Use a new VR study/recording for this participant, linked to the saved rest session. Do not replace the baseline or reuse its recording.
  2. Start MOND collection and Stream to LSL. In iMotions enable MOND_HRV, the established LSL timestamp option, and Shimmer GSR+ at the same 128 Hz setting. Keep source audit data and the optional quality stream. If MOND was restarted, allow its processor and rolling window to fill before the task; mark this setup period separately.
  3. In Study builder, add Screen Recording for the desktop VR mirror if available, or the appropriate supported external-task/scene recording. Name the item VR_TASK. A screen recording captures the mirror, not automatically the headset view or VR events.
  4. Configure researcher-controlled ending if your installed version supports it. Otherwise use a generous technical duration (for example 30 minutes, increased before starting if needed), and mark the actual task end. Verify the available end/advance controls in a pilot. Do not use the rest guide’s 300-second duration or an automatic 15-minute cutoff.
  5. Keep iMotions and MOND running while VR has focus. If the headset is standalone, the Windows sensors must still record; use a tested mirror/cast or external scene view only if your study requires it. Video is context, not necessary for HRV/GSR acquisition.
C

Give the real task a shared timeline

Record VR_START when the actual safety task begins, after instructions and familiarisation. Record VR_END at completion or early termination. Use native iMotions events/annotations or a tested VR → iMotions marker integration. Add hazard, decision, error and help events where possible.

For integration, use the installed iMotions API documentation; this page has no connection to that API. A separate LSL marker stream also needs an inlet and an export test. Verify a shared test event near the start and end, timestamp units, offsets and drift. Keep any latency/uncertainty in the notes. An untested browser timestamp is not a synchronised event.

    A practical marker vocabulary for your safety demo

    Signals need
    their side of the story.

    Record brief ratings before VR and immediately after it. Ask outside the quiet baseline and task windows. Blank means missing, not zero.

    Brief self-reports · exploratory items
    Question / anchorsBefore VRAfter VR
    Stress0 = none · 10 = extreme
    Valence1 = very unpleasant · 9 = very pleasant
    Arousal1 = very calm · 9 = very activated
    VR discomfort / nausea0 = none · 10 = extreme

    These are ad hoc single-item ratings, not validated stress scales, SAM or a cybersickness questionnaire. If your protocol uses a validated instrument, administer its original wording and scoring separately.

    Post-task workload: record the six dimensions.

    Administer the official NASA-TLX instructions consistently, then transcribe its six 0–100 ratings here. Keep performance oriented toward difficulty: 0 = perfect success, 100 = failure. This worksheet reports the unweighted Raw TLX mean only; it does not collect pairwise weights.

    Raw TLX · six-item mean— / 100Enter all six ratings. Missing values are not treated as zero.

    Official NASA-TLX paper package ↗ · NASA discussion of Raw TLX ↗. The overall score includes physical demand; it is not a pure mental-workload measure.

    Keep behavioural outcomes too.

    Enter reviewed counts using your task rubric, not simply the number of browser button clicks.

    Observe activation; investigate its meaning.A change in HRV or GSR may accompany stress, concentration, movement, novelty or discomfort. Combine the signals with ratings, task errors and event context. Higher GSR does not identify a particular emotion; lower RMSSD does not by itself establish higher workload.

    HRV / PULSE INTERVALS

    Same five-minute support

    Recompute mean HR, RMSSD and SDNN from quality-reviewed interval data on native timestamps. Retain gaps; never join intervals across a dropout. Use the same correction version and 25% / 100 ms exploratory allowance in both phases, without tuning it to create a stress effect.

    Report rejected/corrected counts, missing duration and completeness. MOND pulse intervals measure PRV as an HRV proxy; sparse host events yield observed-event estimates. Do not label these as complete beat-to-beat HRV. SDNN requires verified normal beats; otherwise label SDRR. Do not average streamed rolling RMSSD as whole-window RMSSD.

    GSR / ELECTRODERMAL ACTIVITY

    Separate level from responses

    Use calibrated conductance in µS. Review grip/contact artefacts before applying one frozen filtering and tonic/phasic method. Report mean tonic SCL and SCR rate per valid minute, with the same detection threshold and valid-data rules in both phases.

    Keep raw data and the quality mask. Report valid duration; raw SCR counts cannot be compared across unequal task durations. Event-related SCR needs verified event timing and a prespecified response window. Do not treat every peak during a movement as a response to a hazard.

    LF, HF, unequal duration and group analysis

    LF (0.04–0.15 Hz) and HF (0.15–0.40 Hz) may be secondary outputs only when interval completeness, timing, stationarity and comparable five-minute windows support a prespecified spectral method. Record respiration if possible. Do not treat LF/HF as a direct stress or “sympathetic balance” score. Sparse/irregular host updates and active VR may make spectra unsuitable; report unavailable rather than forcing a value.

    If a pause or dropout compromises a fixed window, apply your prespecified quality rule and report it as unavailable when necessary. Do not shift the window to find a cleaner or stronger effect. Keep each participant’s full task and shorter tail. A partial window is not a full five-minute HRV comparison. Do not stretch a 10-minute recording into 15 minutes, concatenate separated clean fragments, or compare whole-task SDNN with a five-minute baseline as though duration had no effect.

    For multiple participants, use paired within-person differences. Report effect sizes and uncertainty; use repeated-measures methods for extra windows, not each window as an independent person. Account for VR experience, completion time, posture and movement. One pair of summary values cannot yield a correlation.
